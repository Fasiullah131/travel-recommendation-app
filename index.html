Recommendation 01

Beaches worth the sunburn

Two coastlines that changed how we think about a beach day.

Seychelles

Anse Source d'Argent

Pink granite boulders, shallow warm water, and a low tide reef walk that takes half a day to cross properly.

Best visited at low tide in the early morning before the day-trip boats arrive from Praslin. Bring reef shoes — the granite is slippery.

Australia

Whitehaven Beach

Seven kilometres of pure silica sand so fine it squeaks underfoot, best seen from the Hill Inlet lookout first.

Only reachable by boat or seaplane from Airlie Beach — there's no road access, which is exactly why it stays this clean.

Recommendation 02

Temples worth an early alarm

Set an alarm before sunrise for these two — the crowds arrive fast.

Cambodia

Angkor Wat

The largest religious monument on earth, and still the most photographed sunrise in Southeast Asia for good reason.

Gates open at 5am. Walk past the reflecting pool crowd to the second pool on the left for a quieter view of the same shot.

Japan

Kinkaku-ji

A gold-leafed pavilion built to sit in its own reflection — small enough to see properly in under an hour.

Visit on a still, windless morning — the reflection is the whole point, and any breeze breaks it.

Recommendation 03

Countries to build a whole trip around

Not a city, not a landmark — these two reward two full weeks each.

East Asia

Japan

Bullet trains make it easy to string together neon cities, quiet mountain onsens, and centuries-old temple towns.

Two weeks is enough for Tokyo, Kyoto, and Hakone without rushing. Buy a JR rail pass before you land — it's cheaper in advance.

Southern Europe

Portugal

Compact enough to cover coast to coast in ten days — Porto's tiled streets, Lisbon's hills, and the Algarve's cliffs.

Rent a car for the Algarve leg — the smaller beach towns aren't well served by the train line.
